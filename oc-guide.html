The 30-second version: An Organized Crime (OC) is a multi-step heist your whole team plays out together. Each role shows you a Checkpoint Pass Rate (CPR) — your personal odds of passing your part. But not every role matters equally: each role also has an invisible weight — how much your CPR in that seat actually moves the whole crew's success chance. A 99% CPR in a 4%-weight role barely helps; a 60% CPR in a 45%-weight role is worth fighting for. Newbie move: don't panic about a mediocre CPR — check this guide's weight table first, because it might be a seat that barely matters.
🌱Day one? Here's the order. Join any OC your faction has open — early-level OCs (1–2) are cheap, forgiving, and the best place to learn the system. Don't stress your CPR number too hard yet: §5 below tells you exactly when a so-so CPR is fine and when it's worth switching roles. Your Crime Experience (see §3) rises just from doing crimes at all, and it's the single biggest lever you have — more useful early on than chasing any one stat.

1The absolute basics

An OC is a faction crime with 3–6 player roles. Everyone joins a slot, spends up to 24h "planning," and once every slot is filled and everyone's healthy and in Torn, the crime plays out automatically as a chain of checkpoints — you don't click anything during execution, the game rolls the dice using the CPR you locked in when you joined.

🛋️This is a hands-off system — don't overthink the "playing" part. Join a role, then just wait out the planning timer; there's nothing to click during it. The only thing required of you is to be in Torn (not hospitalized, jailed, or traveling abroad) once every slot is filled and everyone else is ready too — that's when it actually fires. If you or a teammate isn't ready at that moment, nothing bad happens: the OC simply waits until everyone is. No penalty, no reset, no rush.

Your CPR is shown for every open role before you join it, so you always know your odds going in. It's driven by Crime Experience (CE), your relevant battle/work stats, and your Crime Skill levels — see §3 for exactly which ones matter per role.

🗞️Straight from Torn's own newspaper: "This checkpoint success chance percentage will primarily be affected by their crime experience, however various skills, levels, and achievements throughout Torn will also have a meaningful effect." That's about as official as this system's mechanics get — the rest of this guide is community reverse-engineering on top of that one sentence.

2What "weight" actually means (the one chart that matters)

Here's the thing nobody explains when you first look at an OC: not every role's CPR matters the same amount. Some roles sit on a checkpoint that almost every path through the crime passes through — nail that one and you've basically won. Others sit on a side-branch that barely affects the ending. That's weight: how much a role's CPR actually swings the crew's overall odds.

Illustrative: overall crime success chance as one role's CPR rises, everyone else held steady. Real curves bend with the crime's branching paths rather than running perfectly straight — but the shape holds: steep line = high weight, flat line = low weight.

Real numbers behind that chart: in Stage Fright (level 4), the Sniper role carries ~46% of the whole crime's weight — nearly half. In Market Forces (level 3), Arsonist carries ~4% — you could staff it with your weakest member and barely notice. Same idea, opposite ends of the scale. §6 below has this weight number for every role in every scenario in the game — that's the whole point of this guide.

💡How to actually use this: when your faction is filling an OC, put your highest-CPR members in the highest-weight roles first. A weak CPR in a low-weight seat barely dents your odds — don't waste a strong player there.

3What actually raises your CPR

Every role is gated by CE first — it sets your baseline and caps how much anything else can add. On top of that, specific roles respond to specific stats and Crime Skills:

💪 Muscle / Enforcer
STR + DEF battlestats, plus END. (Disposal Crime Skill was directly tested and ruled out — don't bother training it for this.)
🥷 Thief / Robber
DEX + SPD, plus Shoplifting/Burglary Crime Skills.
🔓 Picklock
DEX, plus Forgery — confirmed by multiple independent players training it from scratch and watching Picklock CPR climb.
🎯 Sniper
DEX + SPD.
💻 Hacker / Techie
Cracking Crime Skill. At level 5+ scenarios, viruses coded gives a small extra bump (caps around 100).
🔧 Engineer / Bomber
Forgery Crime Skill — one player tracked 22→76 Forgery alongside a real Bomber CPR increase.
🗣️ Hustler / Negotiator / Imitator
Hustling + Scamming Crime Skills. Imitator gets roughly 80–90% as much benefit per point as Negotiator does.
🚗 Driver / Hijacker
Racing skill — but Racing alone won't carry the role without real CE behind it too.
🏹 Assassin
Hunting skill.
❤️‍🩹 Reviver
Reviving skill — and it's not a minor role: Reviver carries the single highest weight found anywhere in this guide (46% of Manifest Cruelty).
❔Newer roles with no confirmed driver yet: Interrogator, Saboteur, and Decoy are recent enough that nobody's isolated what trains them through a proper before/after test. If you're staffing one of these, CE and general play are your best bet for now.

Crime Skill doesn't stop mattering at CS100 either — Torn staff confirmed it keeps quietly raising CPR past that point, it just stops showing on your skills page.

4Checkpoints — what a "fail" actually costs you

An OC isn't one big roll — it's a chain of small ones, and not all checkpoints are equally scary:

Mainline
The primary path. Failing shunts you toward a different, usually lower-paying branch — not an instant loss.
Recovery
Your safety net right after a mainline fail — pass this and you're back on track like nothing happened, usually with no reward hit.
Lenient
A mainline check with two recovery chances stacked up. Common near the start of low-level OCs — very forgiving.
Critical
Failing forces a noticeably worse branch (roughly 20–30% less reward). More common in higher-level OCs.
Determinant
Can end the whole OC with nothing. Usually shows up only after you've already failed something earlier.
😮‍💨One fail rarely sinks you. One detailed community account describes an OC surviving four separate checkpoint fails as long as they weren't all back-to-back. It's consecutive fails that tend to be fatal, not any single bad roll.

5Which role should I take?

★ Total newbie, first few OCs? → Just join anything open. Levels 1–2 are cheap and forgiving — this is where you build CE, which pays off everywhere later.

◆ Choosing between two open roles? → Check §6 for that scenario's weights. Take the higher-weight one if your CPR is remotely close between them — that's where your CPR change actually matters most.

▲ Stuck with a low CPR in a role? → Check its weight first. Low CPR in a low-weight role (Arsonist, Lookout, the "extra" Looter/Picklock slots) is often fine — don't burn resources chasing it. Low CPR in a high-weight role (Sniper, Reviver, the lead Imitator/Muscle slot) is worth switching out of if a better-suited teammate is free.

✪ Faction leader / OC organizer? → Sort your roster by CPR, then assign your best CPRs to the highest-weight seats first — every scenario's ranked table is in §6.

One more thing: some duplicate-named slots (Muscle 1 vs. Muscle 2 vs. Muscle 3 in the same crime) can show different CPR for the same player, and different weight too — they're not always interchangeable copies of the same role. Don't assume; check each slot's own number.

7Scope — your faction's fuel for spawning OCs

Scope is what your faction spends to spawn each new OC — caps at 100, regenerates 1/day, and a successful OC refunds a bit extra. To avoid slowly bleeding scope, your team's average CPR needs to clear the break-even rate for whatever tier you're running:

LevelsScope costScope rewardBreak-even success rate
1–21250.0%
3–42366.7%
5–63475.0%
7–84580.0%
8–105683.3%

Most factions can't reliably hit 83% success on the top tier — the standard pattern is farming scope on cheap Level 1–4 OCs, then spending it on the big ones. Chain OCs (Stacking the Deck → Ace in the Hole, and all Level-5-and-up chains) only pay scope on the final stage — multiply each stage's success rate together for your real odds (e.g. 95% × 88% = 83.6% net).

8What's still unknown (be a little skeptical)

⚠Weights may drift with patches. A dev reply once described role-to-checkpoint assignment as "supposed to be completely random" — which would mean these weights are empirical averages that can shift over time. Break the Bank's weights already moved once, and Honey Trap/Leave No Trace appear to have swapped levels entirely. Treat this guide as directional, not gospel.
  • MAN workstat — nobody's figured out what it does for CPR.
  • Battlestat/workstat "caps" (~1B battlestats, ~300k workstats) are widely repeated but also disputed by experienced players as unproven.
  • Mixing high/low CPR teammates — the evidence suggests fails compound through the checkpoint chain rather than there being some separate "team variance penalty," but it's not settled.
  • Two-person checkpoints are believed to be averaged (not OR'd or AND'd), reasoned by elimination — not staff-confirmed.
  • Interrogator, Saboteur, Decoy, and the standalone Pickpocket role have no confirmed skill driver yet — they're too recent for anyone to have run a proper before/after test.

Glossary

OC
Organized Crime — a multi-player faction heist played out automatically over several days.
CPR
Checkpoint Pass Rate — your visible, personal odds of passing your role's checks.
Weight
How much a role's CPR actually swings the whole crime's success chance. High weight = protect it; low weight = don't sweat it.
CE (Crime Experience)
Hidden stat that rises with crimes done. Sets your CPR baseline and caps how much stats/skills can add on top.
Checkpoint
A single pass/fail roll during the crime's execution, tied to one role's CPR.
Scope
Faction resource spent to spawn new OCs. Caps at 100; earned back by succeeding at OCs.
Chain OC
A crime that unlocks another crime instead of paying out directly — only the final stage in the chain rewards you.
Materials
Items some roles need in-inventory to execute. Don't affect CPR — only block the crime from starting if missing.
